# Piquet Cheat Sheet

*A one-page reference. Print `piquet-card.html` for the table card.*

## Setup
2 players · 32 cards (7-8-9-10-J-Q-K-A × 4 suits) · **12 each + 8 talon**.
Talon splits **5 on top (elder's) / 3 beneath (younger's)**.
**Elder** = non-dealer (big advantage) · **Younger** = dealer.
**No trumps, ever.** A *partie* is **6 deals** — three as elder each.

## Order of business
1. Deal 12 + 12, lay the talon **5 / 3**.
2. **Carte blanche** declared (before discarding).
3. **Elder exchanges** (1–5), then **younger** (up to what's left).
4. **Declarations:** point → sequences → sets. Check **repique**.
5. **Play** 12 tricks. Check **pique**, then cards/capot.

## The exchange
- **Elder** *must* take at least **1**, may take up to **5** from the top packet.
  May privately look at any of the 5 left behind.
- **Younger** may take up to what remains (**3**, plus elder's leavings).
- *House rule to settle first:* if younger leaves cards and looks at them,
  elder may see them too.
- Discards are dead. You play 12 either way.

## Declarations — three categories, in this order
**Winner of a category scores ALL their combinations in it; loser scores NOTHING.**
**One card may count in all three** (♠A serves point, quart, and four aces).

### 1. Point — longest suit · scores 1 per card
Ties broken by pips: **A = 11 · K/Q/J/10 = 10 · 9/8/7 = face**.
Equal length *and* equal pips → neither scores.

### 2. Sequences — 3+ in suit-sequence
| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tierce (3) | **3** | Sixième (6) | **16** |
| Quart (4) | **4** | Septième (7) | **17** |
| Quint (5) | **15** | Huitième (8) | **18** |

**⚠ The 4 → 15 cliff is real.** Never break a potential quint to save a trio.
Longest wins; equal length → **higher top card** wins.

### 3. Sets — **10 or higher ONLY** (9s and below never count)
**Quatorze** (four of a kind) = **14** · **Trio** (three) = **3**.
Any quatorze beats any trio; otherwise higher rank wins.

## The dialogue
**Only elder announces.** Younger passes verdict on *elder's* claim — the reply
is never a statement about younger's own hand:

- **"Good"** — your claim stands, you score it. *(Not a confession of weakness —
  younger may hold a quatorze and still say "good" to elder's sequence.)*
- **"Not good"** — I hold better; you score nothing in this category.
- **"Equal"** — for point, elder then states the pip count and younger answers
  again; still equal → neither scores.

Younger never opens a category — younger judges, then declares their own
combinations for whatever categories they won.
You must **show** a scoring combination on demand.

**The verdict leaks.** "Not good" to a point of five tells elder that younger
holds six somewhere, before a card is played. The reply is compulsory.

> **E:** "Point of six." **Y:** "Good." → E scores 6
> **E:** "Quart major." **Y:** "Good." → E scores 4; **Y's quart scores 0**
> **E:** "Nothing else." **Y:** "Three queens — three tens." → Y scores 6

**Sinking:** deliberately declare *less* than you hold (a quart when you have a
quint) to hide your hand for the play. You forfeit the difference. Advanced.

## Bonuses
- **Carte blanche — 10.** No K, Q, or J in your 12. Declare **before discarding**.
- **Repique — 60.** You reach **30+ in declarations alone** while your opponent
  scores **zero** in declarations. (Carte blanche counts toward the 30.)
- **Pique — 30.** You reach **30+ in declarations + tricks combined** before your
  opponent scores **anything**. **Elder only** — younger can't get there, because
  elder scores the instant they lead.

## Play
**Follow suit if you can**; otherwise play anything (it loses). Highest card of
the suit led takes the trick. **Elder leads first**; winner leads next.

| | |
|---|---|
| Lead to a trick | **1** |
| Win a trick you did **not** lead | **1** |
| Win the **last** trick | **+1** |
| **The cards** — take 7+ tricks | **10** |
| **Capot** — take all 12 | **40** (instead of the 10) |

6–6 split scores nothing for the cards.

## Scoring the partie (Rubicon Piquet)
After **6 deals**, compare totals:
- Loser has **100 or more** → winner scores **difference + 100**.
- Loser has **under 100** (**rubiconed**) → winner scores **both totals added
  together + 100**.

*This is why a hopeless loser still fights for every point — crossing 100 can
cost the winner more than the deals did.*

## Elder attacks
1. **Usually take all five.** You see more of the talon than you ever will again.
2. **Discard toward one long runnable suit.** Leads and captures compound; capot
   is worth chasing.
3. **Count your 30** before declaring — a repique is worth more than most hands.
4. Keep an entry so you can *get back in* to run the long suit.

## Younger defends
1. **Realistic goal: 6–6, or steal the majority** when elder overreaches.
2. **Above all, don't get repiqued.** One scoring declaration kills the 60.
3. **Keep guards** — a low card in a suit stops elder running it.
4. **You cannot guard everything.** The whole art of younger's discard is
   choosing which suit to abandon.

## Count the opposing hand
Declarations are a near-complete X-ray — but *each of you chose what to show*.
After the calls, reconstruct their 12 from what was declared, what was denied,
and what the talon must have swallowed. This is the skill the game rests on.
