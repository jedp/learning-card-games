Piquet

Two players · 32 cards · no trumps
A partie is six deals

Setup

Deck A K Q J 10 9 8 7 in each suit. Ace high, 7 low. No trumps, ever.

Deal 12 each. The remaining 8 form the talon: 5 on top (elder's), 3 beneath (younger's).

Elder = non-dealer, and holds a real advantage. Younger = dealer. Six deals to a partie — three as elder each.

Order of business

  1. Deal 12 + 12; lay the talon 5 / 3.
  2. Carte blanche declared — before any discard.
  3. Elder exchanges, then younger.
  4. Declarations: point → sequences → sets. Check repique.
  5. Play twelve tricks. Check pique, then cards / capot.

The exchange

Elder must take at least 1, may take up to 5 from the top packet, and may privately look at any of the five left behind.

Younger may take up to what remains — 3, plus whatever elder declined.

Settle first: if younger leaves cards and looks at them, elder may see them too.

Discards are dead. You play twelve either way.

Declarations

Three categories, in strict order. In each, the better holding scores all of that player's combinations — the loser scores nothing.

One card may count in all three: ♠A serves the point, the quart, and four aces.

The dialogue

Only elder announces. Younger passes verdict on elder's claim — never on their own hand:

“Good”Your claim stands — you score it.
“Not good”I hold better; you score nothing here.
“Equal”Go deeper — see below.

On Equal for the point, elder states the pip count and younger answers again; still equal → neither scores. A scoring combination must be shown on demand.

E: “Point of six.” Y: “Good.” → E 6

E: “Quart major.” Y: “Good.” → E 4; Y's own quart scores 0

E: “Nothing else.” Y: “Three queens — three tens.” → Y 6

Sinking: declare less than you hold — a quart when you have a quint — to hide your hand for the play. You forfeit the difference.

Bonuses

Carte blanche
No K, Q or J in your twelve. Declare before discarding.		10
Repique
You reach 30+ in declarations alone while the opponent scores zero in declarations. Carte blanche counts.		60
Pique
You reach 30+ in declarations and tricks combined before the opponent scores anything. Elder only — elder scores the instant they lead.		 30

Play

Follow suit if you can; otherwise play anything, and it loses. Highest card of the suit led takes the trick. Elder leads first; the winner leads next.

This is why a hopeless loser still fights for scraps: failing to cross 100 can cost more than all six deals.

Elder attacks

  1. Usually take all five. You will never see this much of the talon again.
  2. Discard toward one long, runnable suit. Leads and captures compound, and capot is worth chasing.
  3. Count your 30 before declaring — a repique outweighs most hands.
  4. Keep an entry, so you can get back in and run the long suit.

Younger defends

  1. Realistic goal: 6–6, or steal the majority when elder overreaches.
  2. Above all, don't be repiqued. One scoring declaration kills the 60.
  3. Keep guards — a low card in a suit stops elder running it.
  4. You cannot guard everything. The whole art of younger's discard is choosing which suit to abandon.

Count the hand

The declarations are a near-complete X-ray of your opponent — but each of you chose what to show. Before leading, reconstruct their twelve from what was declared, what was denied, and what the talon must have swallowed.

This is the skill the whole game rests on.
